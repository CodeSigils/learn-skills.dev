Skill to clarify Japanese sentences into a state where their meaning is grasped at a single glance. It activates with prompts such as “make it easy to understand,” “make it readable,” “tidy it up because it’s hard to read,” “remove the AI‑like feel,” “revise the document,” “cut redundant Japanese,” “clarify the writing,” and so on. The target is explanatory Japanese text like technical articles, READMEs, design documents, internal documentation, and blog explanations. Non‑targets include the code itself, short social‑media posts, creative works such as novels and poetry, and the strict language of legal, medical, or academic papers. The goal is to trim away constructions that impose a high reading burden even on native speakers—double negation, long attributive modifiers, chains of “no,” excessive nominalization, redundant expressions, buried enumerations, as well as LLM boilerplate phrases like “the important point is …,” “dig deeper,” and paragraph‑level paraphrase repetitions. Voice, stance, and individuality are not added; the sole aim is clarity (clarity = clear and understandable).
