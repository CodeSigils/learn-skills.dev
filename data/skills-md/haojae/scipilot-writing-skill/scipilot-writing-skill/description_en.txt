The SciPilot Skills family’s academic paper writing and polishing capability is positioned to embed a “top‑journal editor + stringent reviewer” into your writing workflow. It covers the full‑chain writing tasks: Chinese‑English reciprocal polishing, abbreviation/expansion, English/Chinese expression polishing, logic checking, removing AI‑ness (humanize), drafting chapter by chapter (Title/Abstract/Introduction‑CARS/Methods/Results/Discussion/Conclusion/Limitations), generating figure/table captions, experimental results analysis, self‑inspection from a reviewer’s perspective, cover letter (submission letter), and response‑to‑reviewers (rebuttal).

Unlike other “prompt checklists,” this skill includes a **writing‑quality evidence chain**: before each delivery it forcibly runs `scripts/writing_lint.py` for machine self‑check (AI‑fingerprint words, mechanical connectors, dangling ‑ing, dash overuse, negative parallelism, vague attribution, model‑name possessives, LaTeX special‑character escaping, residual Markdown from Word, full‑width Chinese punctuation, passive‑voice ratio, sentence‑length rhythm), then adds an AI‑reader/reviewer perspective self‑audit loop (logic/exaggeration/AI‑ness/consistency → revisions, up to three rounds). Academic‑integrity red line: polishing may adjust expression only, not numbers, direction, or conclusions; never fabricate data or citations; the strength of assertions must match the strength of evidence.

Stage 0 must proactively ask, item by item: task type, document format (.tex/.docx/.md/plain text), target journal or conference, language direction, discipline, degree of conservatism in modifications; silent assumptions are prohibited.
