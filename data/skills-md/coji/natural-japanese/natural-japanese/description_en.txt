The skill of writing and revising Japanese business documents so they are easy to read and understand. It covers the creation and proofreading of business documents such as minutes (including converting transcripts into minutes), research reports, analysis reports, internal guides and manuals, research memos, discussion papers, project plans, proposals, reports, emails, slide outlines, and more, and can be applied to any instruction like “write from the conclusion,” “make the argument clear,” “keep headings concise,” or “explain technical terms in an easy‑to‑understand way.”  

It includes removing an “AI‑like” feel (e.g., “AI‑like,” “AI‑smelling,” “machine‑translation‑like,” “unnatural,” “make it more natural Japanese,” “machine‑like,” “make it sound human,” “monotonous,” phrasing such as “can do” or “could be said,” direct, indirect, and colloquial remarks, and being told or suspected that the text was written by AI), improving hard‑to‑read or confusing sentences (awkward word order, overly long sentences, unclear meaning, misplaced commas, etc.), writing new note articles, blog posts, and essays from scratch on any topic, rewriting and polishing existing texts, diagnosing and scoring AI‑like characteristics (requests such as “Did AI write this?” “Give an AI‑smell score,” “How AI‑like is this?” without rewriting), and accommodating requests to have the model learn one’s personal writing style or be profiled (including having it read past writings and write in one’s own voice).  

It also handles removal of prohibited words, monotony of rhythm, uniform paragraph structure, literal‑translation style from English syntax, and readability principles such as word order, comma placement, one idea per sentence, and appropriate subject‑predicate distance.  

Formatting of technical document chapter structures or Markdown (one‑sentence‑per‑line, block quotes, footnote notation, etc.) is outside the scope—those belong to a different skill set. This skill focuses specifically on the naturalness, readability, and clarity of the text.
