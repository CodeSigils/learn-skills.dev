Trigger this capability when it is necessary to extract legally significant facts from unstructured text such as case descriptions, party statements, chat logs, media reports, etc. Typical trigger scenarios include but are not limited to:
- The user provides a case description in everyday language and requests extraction of legal facts.
- The user asks to convert colloquial, emotional narrative into a fact structure usable for legal analysis.
- The user requests to distinguish "objective facts," "subjective judgments," and "legal evaluations" within the case narrative.
- The user asks to map the extracted facts to the constituent elements of a specific legal relationship.
- The user requests to organize a case timeline or create a fact‑scrutiny table.
- The user asks to transform everyday language into legal terminology (e.g., "He keeps dragging his feet and doesn't repay" → "Failure to perform after the performance deadline has elapsed").
The core value of this capability: to organize mixed everyday narratives into a fact structure that can be fed into legal reasoning, which is a prerequisite step for legal analysis.
